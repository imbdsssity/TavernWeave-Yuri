# The Lantern Archive

**Status: Illustrative, clearly synthetic source fixture; not observed test evidence. Revision 1.** All people, places and events below are invented for later bounded continuity trials. This is source-fixture authoring only: no checkpoint, model response, completed trial or observed result is included. It contains four short chapters in the declared order C1 → C2 → C3 → C4. Paragraph IDs are stable evidence locators within this edition, volume V1.

## C1 — The returned key

**C1.P1** My name is Iris Vale. I am a twenty-seven-year-old woman, hired to restore the notebooks in Bellwater's lantern archive. On Monday afternoon I met Nera Moss, the thirty-year-old woman who maintained the building. She showed me the workroom but left the north cabinet locked.

**C1.P2** A note on my desk read, “The spare key is back. — Rook.” I did not know who Rook was. Nera pocketed a brass key while I unpacked. Perhaps she had just collected the returned spare; perhaps it was another key. I could not tell.

**C1.P3** Nera asked whether I would join her for tea after work. I wanted to accept, but the chapter ends before I answered. We were new colleagues, and I had no evidence that she meant a date.

## C2 — An uncertain witness

**C2.P1** On Tuesday morning the gatekeeper told me, “Rook came through at dawn.” Then she frowned. The hallway clock had stopped, and she was no longer sure when the visitor had arrived. She had heard footsteps, not seen a face.

**C2.P2** I imagined Rook was a separate courier. That was my guess, not something anyone had confirmed. A rumor in town said someone had sold the north cabinet's notebooks. When I asked Nera, she said, “I didn't sell the notebooks.” She offered to show me the restoration ledger the next day.

## C3 — The volunteer name

**C3.P1** On Wednesday Nera opened the volunteer ledger with me. Its current signature read “Nera Moss — volunteer name: Rook.” She explained that she had used the name for years. The note on my desk was hers; Rook was not another courier.

**C3.P2** A dated receipt and the cabinet inventory showed that the notebooks had been loaned to the town conservator for repairs, not sold. Nera had returned the spare key on Monday before I arrived. The key I saw her pocket was her own workroom key.

**C3.P3** The gatekeeper corrected her visitor entry after checking her shift notes: the footsteps had been on Monday evening, not Tuesday at dawn. Neither the clock nor her first estimate established the visitor's identity. I crossed out my courier theory, keeping a note of why I had believed it.

## C4 — A later invitation

**C4.P1** On Friday the repaired notebooks came back. Nera told me, “Monday's tea was meant as a welcome for a colleague. Tonight, if you'd like, I'm asking you on a date.” Her new invitation did not change what she had meant on Monday.

**C4.P2** I accepted the Friday invitation. We agreed to meet after closing. That agreement belonged to Friday; at Monday's unanswered invitation, I knew neither Rook's identity nor the reason the notebooks were missing.
